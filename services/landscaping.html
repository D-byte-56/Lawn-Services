Landscaping that starts with the ground.

Landscaping

Groundwork first, then a yard that lasts.

A good-looking yard isn't just what you plant — it's the shape of the ground underneath it. If the grade runs the wrong way or the drainage is off, water pools where it shouldn't, beds wash out, and new sod struggles no matter how much you water it.

We start where it counts: correcting the grade so water moves away from the house, then building the lawn and beds on top of ground that's ready for them. Sod or seed, plant beds and mulch, and the seasonal cleanups that keep it all looking cared-for through the year.

Around Cumming and Dawsonville the soil is heavy clay and the summer rain comes down hard — which is exactly why the groundwork matters more here than most people expect.

Grading & drainage

Get the water moving the right way.

Standing water and a yard that never dries out usually trace back to one thing: the grade. When the ground slopes toward the house instead of away from it, every hard rain pushes water where you don't want it — against the foundation, into low spots, across the beds. We re-shape the grade so runoff drains away cleanly, correct the low areas that collect water, and set the yard up so what you plant next actually has a chance.

  • Grading to move water away from the house
  • Correcting low spots and pooling
  • Erosion control on slopes
  • A base that's ready for sod and beds
Sod, beds & mulch

A finished yard, not just a planted one.

Once the ground is right, we build the yard on top of it. Sod gives you a lawn the day it goes down and holds the soil on slopes; seeding is the cheaper route on big, flat areas when you can wait for it to fill in. Then come the beds — shaped, edged, planted, and topped with mulch that holds moisture, keeps weeds down, and protects roots through the summer heat and winter cold.

Why the groundwork pays off

Grading and drainage done right raise what your home is worth and head off the expensive problems — erosion, pooling water, and a soggy yard — before they start. In North Georgia clay, that base is what makes the difference between a yard that lasts and one you're redoing in two years.

Questions

Landscaping, answered.

Why does drainage matter so much in North Georgia?
Our soil is heavy red clay, and it doesn't absorb water the way sandy soil does. Add the hard summer downpours we get around Cumming and Dawsonville and water pools where it shouldn't — against the foundation, in low spots, over the septic field. Correct grading sends that water away from the house and keeps it from washing out your beds and lawn.
Sod or seed — which should I choose?
Sod gives you a finished lawn the day it goes down and holds the soil right away, which is why it's the better choice on slopes and anywhere you want it looking done now. Seed costs less and works well on large, flat areas when you can wait for it to fill in. We'll tell you which one fits your yard and budget.
Do you install the beds and mulch too?
Yes. We shape and edge the beds, set the plants, and top it off with mulch that holds moisture and keeps weeds down. Fresh mulch also protects roots through the heat of summer and the cold snaps in winter.
Can you handle a full property cleanup?
We do. Seasonal cleanups — leaves, spent growth, overgrown beds, and hauling it all off — get a property back in shape in spring and fall. It pairs well with a fresh round of mulch and any bed work that's due.
